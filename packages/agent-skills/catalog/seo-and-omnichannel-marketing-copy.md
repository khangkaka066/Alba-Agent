## Purpose
Create useful search and campaign content aligned with intent, brand voice, and verified product claims.

## Standard operating procedure
1. Identify audience, funnel stage, search intent, geography, channel, conversion goal, and brand voice. Separate informational, comparison, and transactional queries.
2. Map each target keyword to one page or content asset. Group close variants by intent instead of repeating keywords mechanically.
3. Inspect the supplied product facts and approved claims. Mark unsupported differentiators, performance promises, pricing, or regulated claims for review.
4. Build a clear hook, useful story or proof, and specific offer or next step. Adapt length, CTA, and tone for each channel while preserving one core message.
5. Write a helpful title, descriptive headings, concise meta description, internal-link suggestions, and accessible alt text when relevant.
6. Review for clarity, originality, factual accuracy, brand consistency, and spam signals.

## Checklist
- [ ] Search intent and audience are explicit.
- [ ] Each keyword maps to a distinct useful purpose.
- [ ] Claims are sourced or approved.
- [ ] Channel-specific copy feels native, not copy-pasted.
- [ ] CTA is clear and appropriate to the user's stage.

## Guardrails
Never fabricate testimonials, rankings, product capabilities, discounts, or urgency. Avoid keyword stuffing, deceptive claims, and manipulative dark patterns.

## Output template
### Intent and audience
Target user, query cluster, and funnel stage.

### Core message
Value proposition and substantiated proof.

### Channel assets
Headline, body, CTA, meta description, and adaptations.

### Review notes
Claims requiring approval and suggested measurements.

## Golden example
For a comparison query, answer the evaluation criteria directly, disclose meaningful limitations, and use a factual comparison table rather than repeating the keyword in every heading.
